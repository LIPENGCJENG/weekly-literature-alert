## 1. Asymmetric Ultrathin Salt-Rich Composite Polymer Electrolyte Formed by Spin Coating for High-Performance Lithium Metal Batteries

**Authors**: Shuaitian Jia, Linxin Yang, Yanpei Wang, Li Zhang, Langli Luo
**Journal/Platform**: ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 8 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-07-09
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.6c08312](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.6c08312)
**Relevance Score**: 5.07/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The study addresses the inherent limitations of conventional composite polymer electrolytes in solid-state lithium metal batteries, specifically low room-temperature ionic conductivity, suboptimal lithium-ion transference numbers, and poor mechanical/electrochemical interfacial compatibility.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to have developed an asymmetric, spin-coated composite electrolyte featuring a PVDF framework and a sulfolane-rich PEO/LiTFSI cathode-side layer. They assert that this architecture, combined with LLTO fillers, simultaneously optimizes Li+ transference numbers (0.78) and interfacial impedance while suppressing dendrite growth through a high-modulus anode-side interface.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that their asymmetric electrolyte design successfully balances the conflicting requirements of high ionic conductivity at the cathode and mechanical stability at the anode, as evidenced by the reported 82.5% capacity retention over 500 cycles in LFP/Li full cells.

## 4. Atomistic Insights into the Crucial Role of Lithium-Ion Coordination on the Emergence of Different Chain-Length and Salt-Concentration Regimes in PEO–LiTFSI Electrolytes

**Authors**: Andreas Thum, David Bienek, Diddo Diddens, Andreas Heuer
**Journal/Platform**: Macromolecules
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 5.7 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-07-12
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.macromol.6c00251](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.macromol.6c00251)
**Relevance Score**: 2.62/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The study addresses the lack of a comprehensive, systematic understanding of how lithium-ion transport in PEO–LiTFSI electrolytes is influenced by the interplay between polymer chain length and salt concentration across a wide parameter space.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors provide a large-scale molecular dynamics simulation dataset that identifies three distinct chain-length regimes and two salt-concentration regimes, specifically linking these regimes to changes in lithium-ion coordination environments and the transition between center-of-mass motion and segmental dynamics.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that lithium-ion transport is governed by a crossover in coordination chemistry: at low chain lengths, transport is sensitive to specific coordination motives, while at high chain lengths, it becomes independent of chain size due to a shift toward segmental dynamics. Furthermore, they conclude that the transition from 'salt-in-polymer' to 'polymer-in-salt' regimes is driven by the shift from PEO-dominated to mixed PEO/TFSI lithium-ion coordination.

## 5. Mechanism-Guided Design of Conductive and Stable Interlayers for NASICON-Based Solid-State Lithium Batteries

**Authors**: Jheng‐Yi Huang, Pavitra Srivastava, Yoshiya Matsuoka, Yun-Ping Chang, Yan-Ming Lai, Behrouz Bazri et al.
**Journal/Platform**: ACS Applied Energy Materials
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 5.5 / Q2(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-07-09
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1021/acsaem.6c01337](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsaem.6c01337)
**Relevance Score**: 2.61/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The chemical instability of NASICON-type LAGP solid electrolytes when in direct contact with lithium metal, specifically the reduction of Ge4+ and the subsequent formation of resistive interfacial layers that impede battery performance.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors propose a thin (8 µm) composite polymer electrolyte (CPE) interlayer consisting of Ta-doped garnet (LLZTO) and succinonitrile in a PEO matrix to physically decouple the LAGP from the lithium anode and regulate ion transport.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The CPE interlayer enables stable cycling in symmetric cells for over 300 hours with a 75 mV overpotential and maintains 90% capacity in full cells; molecular dynamics simulations suggest that the garnet fillers facilitate Li-salt dissociation and modulate Li+ mobility to stabilize the interface.
