## 1. Simulation of the Effect of Filler Particles on the Conductivity of a Polymer Electrolyte During Compression Loading

**Authors**: Matthew D. Suski
**Journal/Platform**: OpenAlex
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 50.5 / JCR not available(配置表)
**Publication Date**: 2026-07-27
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.31979/etd.3dqs-sugk](https://doi.org/10.31979/etd.3dqs-sugk)
**Relevance Score**: 4.86/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The lack of understanding regarding how mechanical compression affects the ionic conductivity of polymer electrolytes (specifically PEO-LiTFSI) and whether the addition of ceramic filler particles (LLZTO) can mitigate performance degradation under such loading conditions.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The author provides experimental evidence that ionic conductivity in PEO-LiTFSI decreases under compression and that the addition of 10 wt% LLZTO filler reduces this conductivity loss. Additionally, the author presents finite element method (FEM) simulations intended to model these mechanical-electrochemical interactions.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The study concludes that mechanical compression negatively impacts the ionic conductivity of PEO-LiTFSI, and that incorporating LLZTO filler particles provides a stabilizing effect. While the simulations qualitatively support the experimental observation that fillers reduce conductivity sensitivity to compression, the author acknowledges a quantitative discrepancy where the simulated change in slope is smaller than the experimental results.

## 4. Atomic and Molecular Structure Regulated In Situ Cross-Linked Polyurethane Gel Electrolyte for High-Performance Lithium Metal Batteries

**Journal/Platform**: Nano-Micro Letters
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 38.5 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-08-03
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1007/s40820-026-02307-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40820-026-02307-4)
**Relevance Score**: 1.93/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The incompatibility of conventional liquid electrolytes with high-voltage cathodes and lithium metal anodes, which leads to poor electrochemical stability and unstable solid electrolyte interphase formation in lithium metal batteries.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to have developed an in situ cross-linked polyurethane gel electrolyte (G-P3 AR) that utilizes atomic and molecular structure regulation—specifically polyester segments for high-voltage stability, polyether segments for reduced desolvation barriers, and sp2-hybridized boron for anion immobilization—to improve electrochemical performance.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

## 5. Moisture-induced surface degradation mechanism of argyrodite Li6PS5Cl under dry-room conditions

**Authors**: Yoon‐Seong Kim, Jeongdoo Yi, Sihyeon Sung, Dong‐Hwa Seo
**Journal/Platform**: Nature Communications
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 18.9 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-07-31
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75537-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75537-0)
**Relevance Score**: 0.95/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The rapid surface degradation of the solid electrolyte Li6PS5Cl (LPSC) when exposed to trace moisture in industrial dry-room environments, which leads to reduced ionic conductivity and hinders the scalability of all-solid-state battery manufacturing.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to have elucidated a five-step atomic-scale mechanism of moisture-induced degradation, identifying the specific sequence of chemical reactions—from H2O adsorption and P-S bond weakening to the formation of a porous, O-enriched surface layer—using a combination of first-principles calculations and depth-profiling XPS.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The study concludes that the degradation process is driven by the rotation of O-substituted PS4 tetrahedra and subsequent phase separation into LiCl, Li3PO4, Li2SO4, LiOH, and Li2CO3, resulting in a 36% decrease in ionic conductivity over three days. The authors propose that mitigating this requires strategies focused on polyhedral-rigidity tuning and moisture-blocking surface modifications.
