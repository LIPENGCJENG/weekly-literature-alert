## 1. Halide Solid‐State Electrolytes for all‐Solid‐State Lithium Batteries: Structure, Transport, Interface Relationships and Design Principles

**Authors**: Zijian Zhang, Yanan Xu, Xudong Zhang, Qifan Peng, Xiong Zhang, Xianzhong Sun et al.
**Journal/Platform**: Advanced Energy Materials
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 26 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-08-08
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.71414](https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.71414)
**Relevance Score**: 8.3/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The lack of a comprehensive, fundamental understanding of the structure-transport relationships and interfacial behaviors in halide solid-state electrolytes (HSEs), which hinders their rational design and practical application in all-solid-state lithium batteries.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors provide a systematic review correlating crystal chemistry, defect chemistry, and lattice dynamics with Li+ transport properties, while critically comparing trivalent, divalent, and mixed-valence halide systems to establish design principles for improved ionic conductivity and interfacial stability.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that achieving high-performance HSEs requires a multi-faceted approach involving aliovalent doping, amorphization, and lattice softening, while emphasizing that future research must prioritize scalable synthesis, moisture stability, and the mitigation of chemo-mechanical degradation at electrode interfaces.

## 4. Li <sub>6.4</sub> La <sub>3</sub> Zr <sub>1.4</sub> Ta <sub>0.6</sub> O <sub>12</sub> ‐Reinforced Hybrid Eutectic Polymer Electrolyte With Balancing Ionic Conductivity and Mechanical Strength for Lithium Metal Batteries

**Authors**: Kai Yu, Huipeng Zeng, Chunyu Liu, Yifei Qin, Zhenyao Wei, Hongli Xu et al.
**Journal/Platform**: Battery energy
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 11.1 / Q3(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-08-05
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1002/bte2.70146](https://doi.org/10.1002/bte2.70146)
**Relevance Score**: 2.89/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The inherent trade-off between ionic conductivity and mechanical strength in solid-state electrolytes, often referred to as the 'solidification penalty,' which limits the performance and safety of lithium metal batteries.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to have developed a Li6.4La3Zr1.4Ta0.6O12 (LLZTO)-reinforced hybrid eutectic polymer electrolyte (HEE@PF) that simultaneously improves ionic conductivity and mechanical puncture strength. They further claim that the LLZTO filler acts as a multifunctional component that promotes LiTFSI dissociation, creates ion-conducting pathways, and modulates the solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) composition.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The study concludes that the HEE@PF electrolyte achieves a room-temperature ionic conductivity of 0.484 mS cm-1 and a puncture strength of 5.5 N. Based on the reported electrochemical testing, the authors conclude that the electrolyte enables stable cycling in Li||Li symmetric cells and Li||LFP full cells, suggesting that transforming passive ceramic fillers into active components is a viable strategy for high-performance lithium metal batteries.
