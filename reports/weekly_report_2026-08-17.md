**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 26 / Q1(EasyScholar)

**Relevance Score**: 1.3/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The instability of the solid-solid interface in sulfide-based all-solid-state batteries (ASSBs) under low stack pressure, which leads to poor contact and uneven lithium deposition.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors introduce a hybrid interlayer composed of a mechanically compliant MXene framework integrated with dual-function lithiophilic seeds (immobile Si and mobile Ag) to manage interfacial stress and regulate lithium nucleation kinetics.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The study concludes that the synergy between the MXene framework's mechanical adaptability and the dual-seed lithiophilic regulation enables stable cycling of Li-metal anodes in sulfide ASSBs under low stack pressure (0.21 MPa), with reported performance metrics of 72.4% capacity retention over 200 cycles.

## 4. Surface‐Fluorinated Conductive Carbon for Coupled Electron/Ion Transport Through the Graphite Anode

**Journal/Platform**: Advanced Energy Materials
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 26 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-08-15
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.71436](https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.71436)
**Relevance Score**: 1.3/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The sluggish lithium-ion transport kinetics and high interfacial polarization in graphite anodes during high-rate charging, which are exacerbated by conventional conductive carbons that provide electronic pathways but fail to facilitate ion transport.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors propose a gas-phase fluorination strategy to transform standard conductive carbon into surface-fluorinated conductive carbon (F-SP). They claim this material enables coupled electron/ion transport and facilitates the in situ formation of a LiF-rich interphase, which they posit regulates interfacial chemistry and improves charge-transfer kinetics.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that surface-fluorinated conductive carbon acts as a multifunctional kinetic regulator, resulting in reduced polarization and improved rate capability in graphite anodes. The reported electrochemical data for NCM523||Gr/F-SP full-cells supports enhanced capacity retention and rate performance compared to standard graphite anodes under the tested conditions.

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The study addresses the inherent limitations of PEO-based solid-state electrolytes, specifically low ionic conductivity, low lithium-ion transference numbers, and poor interfacial stability, which collectively impede the performance of all-solid-state lithium metal batteries at near-room and sub-zero temperatures.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to introduce an 'electrostatic suspension' strategy using Mg1-xLixF2 fillers in a PEO matrix, which purportedly creates dynamically balanced electrostatic fields at the filler/polymer interface to facilitate rapid lithium-ion transport. They further claim this mechanism, combined with an in situ formed LiF-rich solid electrolyte interphase, enables stable battery operation at 0°C.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that the composite electrolyte achieves an ionic conductivity of 0.158 mS cm-1 and a transference number of 0.56 at 30°C. They conclude that the electrostatic suspension mechanism successfully enables stable cycling for over 2650 hours at 0°C in symmetric cells and maintains 98% capacity retention after 300 cycles in full cells, suggesting the strategy is effective for wide-temperature-range battery applications.
