1. Li3InCl6 as an Active Filler Improves Interfacial Stability of PEO-LiTFSI Composite Solid Polymer Electrolytes for All-Solid-State Lithium Metal Batteries

Authors: Hongrui Kang, Weiye Ma, Jan P. Hofmann, Zhonghui Gao Journal/Platform: ACS Omega SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile: 5.2 / Q2(EasyScholar) Publication Date: 2026-08-21 DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.6c02071 Relevance Score: 7.26/10

1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The lack of fundamental understanding regarding how different classes of inorganic fillers (oxide, sulfide, and halide) influence the interfacial stability and chemical evolution between composite solid polymer electrolytes (CSPEs) and lithium metal anodes.

2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim that halide-based fillers (Li3InCl6) uniquely facilitate the formation of a passivating, electron-rich Li–O and Li–S interfacial layer, which they propose is superior to oxide or sulfide fillers for stabilizing the lithium metal interface.

3. What are its main conclusions?

The study concludes that Li3InCl6 acts as an active filler that improves interfacial stability and reduces interfacial impedance, thereby increasing the critical current density for lithium dendrite formation, supported by XPS analysis and impedance spectroscopy.

2. Integrated multicomponent design strategies to enhance the electrochemical performance of solid-state lithium metal batteries on interfacial stability of composite polymer electrolytes

Authors: Shih-Wen Hsieh, Yi–Shiuan Wu, Jeng-Kuei Chang, Rajan Jose, Nithyadharseni Palaniyandy, Wen‐Chen Chien et al. Journal/Platform: Journal of Energy Storage SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile: 10.7 / Q1(EasyScholar) Publication Date: 2026-08-21 DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.est.2026.124336 Relevance Score: 5.2/10

1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The study addresses the dual challenges of sluggish ion transport and poor interfacial stability in all-solid-state lithium metal batteries (ASSLMBs) caused by the inherent limitations of conventional polymer electrolytes.

2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to have developed a multifunctional composite polymer electrolyte (CPE) using a synergistic, multiscale design that integrates a crosslinked PEGDA network, dual-Li salts, polydopamine-modified LLZAO ceramic fillers, and multiple functional additives (FEC, SUL, Mg(TFSI)2) to simultaneously regulate solvation structure and interfacial chemistry.

3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that the integrated design improves electrochemical performance, reporting an ionic conductivity of 1.81 × 10^-4 S cm^-1, a Li+ transference number of 0.38, and an electrochemical stability window of 5.21 V. They further conclude that the electrolyte enables stable cycling in symmetric cells for 600 hours and maintains 95% capacity retention in LiFePO4 full cells over 600 cycles.

3. Synergistic enhancement of ionic conductivity and cycling stability in tetracyanoethylene added PEO–LiTDI solid polymer electrolytes

Authors: Pramod K. Singh, Anji Reddy Polu, Deepak Kumar, Kuldeep Mishra, Aseel A. Kareem, Xungang Diao et al. Journal/Platform: Scientific Reports SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile: 4.9 / Q1(EasyScholar) Publication Date: 2026-08-18 DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-67297-0 Relevance Score: 4.91/10

1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The study addresses the inherent limitations of poly(ethylene oxide) (PEO)-based solid polymer electrolytes, specifically their low ionic conductivity at room temperature and poor electrochemical stability during cycling, which hinder their practical application in solid-state lithium batteries.

2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim that the incorporation of tetracyanoethylene (TCNE) as an additive into PEO–LiTDI electrolytes creates a synergistic effect that improves lithium-ion transport kinetics and enhances the stability of the electrode-electrolyte interface.

3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that TCNE modification facilitates higher ionic conductivity and superior cycling performance compared to pristine PEO–LiTDI systems, attributing these improvements to modified ion-polymer coordination and stabilized interface formation.

The study addresses the limitations of conventional liquid electrolytes in lithium batteries, specifically focusing on the poor interfacial contact and low ionic conductivity associated with solid-state electrolytes, particularly those utilizing Li1.3Al0.3Ti1.7(PO4)3 (LATP) ceramic fillers.

2. What contribution does it claim?

3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that the composite electrolyte architecture improves ionic conductivity and interfacial stability, resulting in enhanced electrochemical performance for quasi-solid-state lithium battery systems.

5. Screen-Printed Li Arrays with Reinforced Interphase as Ultrathin Anodes for High-Energy–Density Liquid- and Solid-State Batteries

Authors: Wenting Liu, Zhenyao Wei, De Ning, Zhenhuan Lu, Fengping Zhang, Xuhao Wang et al. Journal/Platform: Nano-Micro Letters SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile: 38.5 / Q1(EasyScholar) Publication Date: 2026-08-21 DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40820-026-02342-1 Relevance Score: 1.93/10

1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The difficulty of fabricating ultrathin lithium metal anodes due to poor molten lithium wettability on current collectors, and the subsequent electrochemical instability caused by inhomogeneous lithium plating and active lithium consumption during cycling.

2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to have developed a screen-printing strategy using a molten salt-derived lithium nanocomposite to create mask-patterned discrete lithium arrays, which purportedly enables the fabrication of ultrathin anodes (below 30 μm) and promotes a more uniform solid electrolyte interphase.

3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that their screen-printed lithium array anodes improve electrochemical performance in both liquid- and solid-state battery systems, specifically reporting stable cycling for 260 cycles and high energy density metrics of 504 Wh kg⁻¹ and 1071 Wh L⁻¹.
