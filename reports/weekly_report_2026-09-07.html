1. Interface Engineering via Ceria Coating Reconciles Ion Transport and Lithium Compatibility in Composite Polymer Electrolytes

Authors: Quanzhi Lin, Senlin Liao, Weixian Wang, Qi Zeng, Yajie Yang Journal/Platform: Advanced Energy Materials SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile: 26 / Q1(EasyScholar) Publication Date: 2026-09-05 DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.71551 Relevance Score: 5.97/10

1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The study addresses the performance limitations of composite polymer electrolytes (CPEs) in solid-state lithium-metal batteries, specifically the poor compatibility between ceramic fillers and polymer matrices, and the deleterious side reactions occurring at the interface between ceramic fillers and lithium metal anodes.

2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to introduce a multifunctional ceria (CeO2) interfacial coating on ceramic fillers to simultaneously improve the dispersion of fillers within the polymer matrix, facilitate lithium-ion transport, and act as a physical barrier to prevent direct contact between fillers and lithium metal.

3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that the ceria-coated filler modification enhances ionic conductivity (0.772 mS cm-1), increases the Li-ion transference number (0.626), and extends the electrochemical stability window (5.03 V). They further conclude that this interface engineering effectively suppresses interfacial side reactions, as evidenced by stable cycling in symmetric cells (1500 h) and performance retention in Li|NCM811 pouch cells.
