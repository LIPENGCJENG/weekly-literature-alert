1. Enhancing Interfacial Stability Between NASICON‐Type Solid‐State Electrolyte and Lithium Metal Anode via Multifunctional Synergistic Interface Engineering

Authors: Jian Liu, Liu Pei, Ying Song, Na Wang, Yejing Li, Xindong Wang et al. Journal/Platform: Small SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile: 12.1 / Q1(EasyScholar) Publication Date: 2026-09-12 DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/smll.75686 Relevance Score: 5.27/10

1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The poor interfacial contact, uncontrolled lithium dendrite growth, and deleterious side reactions occurring at the interface between NASICON-type LATP solid-state electrolytes and lithium metal anodes.

2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim the development of a multifunctional PVGa composite interlayer (PVDF-HFP/LiTFSI/GaF3) that facilitates in situ formation of a LiF/LixGa hybrid solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) to improve interfacial stability and ion transport.

3. What are its main conclusions?

The study concludes that the PVGa interlayer effectively homogenizes lithium deposition and suppresses side reactions, resulting in improved critical current density and extended cycling stability in symmetric cells and LFP-based full cells.

2. Interfacial Anion-Tethering and Coordination-Induced Polymer Electrolytes for Zinc Ion Batteries

Authors: Noreen Ali, Yu Zhang, Yi Li, Shunyu Yao, Xirui Kong, Ben Chong et al. Journal/Platform: Energy Material Advances SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile: 17.4 / Q1(EasyScholar) Publication Date: 2026-09-14 DOI: https://doi.org/10.34133/energymatadv.0646 Relevance Score: 3.2/10

1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The paper addresses the inherent limitations of polymer electrolytes in zinc-ion batteries, specifically the sluggish ion transport kinetics, poor interfacial compatibility with zinc anodes, and the lack of structural stability that leads to dendrite growth and parasitic side reactions.

2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to develop a composite polymer electrolyte utilizing an interfacial anion-tethering and coordination-induced strategy. They propose that this design facilitates rapid Zn2+ transport pathways and enhances the electrochemical stability of the electrode-electrolyte interface.

3. What are its main conclusions?

The study concludes that the engineered electrolyte effectively suppresses zinc dendrite formation and mitigates side reactions, resulting in improved cycling stability and rate capability for zinc-ion batteries compared to conventional polymer electrolyte systems.

3. Polar molecule-derived segmental motion in polymer solid electrolyte and integrated electrode architecture enable stable silicon anodes

Authors: Wang X. Journal/Platform: Journal of Energy Chemistry SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile: 15.6 / Q1(EasyScholar) Publication Date: 2026-09-01 DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jechem.2026.05.059 Relevance Score: 3.11/10

1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The mechanical instability and poor interfacial contact between silicon anodes and polymer solid electrolytes, which lead to rapid capacity decay and limited cycle life in all-solid-state lithium-ion batteries.

2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to have developed a polymer solid electrolyte incorporating polar molecule-derived segmental motion and an integrated electrode architecture to enhance ion transport and interfacial adhesion.

3. What are its main conclusions?

The study concludes that the engineered segmental motion within the polymer matrix and the integrated architecture effectively accommodate the volume expansion of silicon anodes, resulting in improved electrochemical stability and cycling performance.

4. Anion‐Programmed Lewis‐Acidity‐Amplified Cellulose Separators for Dual Interphase Regulation in Li||NCM811 Batteries

Authors: Zihan Guo, Zichan Yuan, Rongfu Xu, Huang Yi, Ningxin Chen, Zhaohui Wang Journal/Platform: Advanced Materials SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile: 29.6 / Q1(EasyScholar) Publication Date: 2026-09-13 DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.75004 Relevance Score: 1.48/10

1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The instability of electrode-electrolyte interfaces in lithium metal batteries, specifically the limitations of passive separators that fail to actively regulate ion transport and interphase formation at both the lithium metal anode and the NCM811 cathode.

2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to have developed a 'ZrF-CNF' separator that utilizes anion-programmed Lewis acid-base engineering to amplify Lewis acidity. They assert this design facilitates Li+ desolvation and PF6- enrichment, thereby enabling the formation of LiF-rich interphases on both electrodes.

3. What are its main conclusions?

The study concludes that the ZrF-CNF separator improves electrochemical performance, evidenced by stable cycling in Li||Li cells (2400 h), capacity retention in Li||NCM811 cells (80.1% over 800 cycles), and high energy density in pouch cell configurations (383.1 Wh kg-1).
