## 1. Unveiling the Interfacial Dynamics of Crosslinked Biodegradable Polycaprolactone Solid Polymer Electrolytes and Li Metal During Lithium Plating and Stripping via Operando ATR‐FTIR Spectroscopy

**Authors**: Jian‐Fen Wang, Matthias Weiling, Felix Pfeiffer, Marian Cristian Stan, Martin Winter, Masoud Baghernejad
**Journal/Platform**: Advanced Science
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 15.6 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-09-16
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.77788](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.77788)
**Relevance Score**: 5.45/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The lack of mechanistic understanding regarding the interfacial chemical evolution and degradation processes occurring between crosslinked polycaprolactone (PCL)-based solid polymer electrolytes and lithium metal anodes during electrochemical cycling.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to provide real-time, operando insights into the interfacial dynamics of cl-PCL electrolytes using ATR-FTIR spectroscopy, specifically identifying the formation of ordered PCL domains and TFSI- anion redistribution, and demonstrating that crosslinking improves cycling stability compared to non-crosslinked PCL.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The study concludes that crosslinking enhances the mechanical stability of PCL electrolytes, and that the interface undergoes dynamic chemical changes—including polymer degradation and anion migration—which can be visualized through penetration depth-dependent spectral mapping.

## 2. Electronic‐State Continuity Across a Heterointerphase of High‐Capacity Ni‐Rich Cathode for Stable Sulfide‐Based All‐Solid‐State Lithium Batteries

**Authors**: Zhuolin Yang, Yuanxing Zhang, Lirui Luo, Yawen Liu, Haijian Lv, Xinyu Zhang et al.
**Journal/Platform**: Advanced Materials
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 29.6 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-09-21
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.75070](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.75070)
**Relevance Score**: 3.81/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The study addresses the dual challenges of interfacial chemical instability and sluggish lithium-ion kinetics at the cathode-electrolyte interface in sulfide-based all-solid-state lithium batteries using nickel-rich layered oxide cathodes.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to introduce a design principle based on 'electronic-state continuity' achieved through co-doping with period-5 elements (Y, Zr, Nb, Mo). They claim this creates a self-assembled, functionally differentiated heterointerphase that simultaneously passivates the surface and facilitates charge redistribution to mitigate space-charge barriers.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that the engineered heterointerphase successfully suppresses side reactions and accelerates Li+ transport, resulting in improved electrochemical performance, specifically a capacity of 204.4 mAh g-1 at 0.1 C and 86.7% retention over 1000 cycles at 0.5 C in a Li6PS5Cl-based system.

## 3. Dipole–dipole interaction facilitates anion-rich solvation structures in polymer electrolytes for solid-state lithium metal batteries

**Journal/Platform**: Science Advances
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 14.8 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-09-18
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aej0175](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aej0175)
**Relevance Score**: 3.07/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The limited electrochemical stability and poor interfacial compatibility of conventional polymer electrolytes, which lead to lithium dendrite growth and rapid capacity degradation in high-energy-density lithium metal batteries.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim that incorporating pentafluorophenyl acrylate into the polymer backbone induces dipole-dipole interactions that modulate Li+ solvation, specifically promoting an anion-rich primary coordination sphere to facilitate a LiF-dominated solid electrolyte interphase.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The study concludes that dipole-mediated solvation engineering effectively suppresses dendrite formation and improves interfacial stability, as evidenced by extended cycling performance in symmetric cells and high-energy-density pouch cell configurations.

## 5. Polymer-Ionic Liquid-TiO2 Composite Electrolyte for Safe and High-Voltage Lithium Metal Batteries

**Authors**: Yixin Wei, Jiaying Chen, Yaowu Cai, Qiankun Shi, Guosheng Shao, Ruohan Hou et al.
**Journal/Platform**: ACS Applied Polymer Materials
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 5.2 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-09-19
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1021/acsapm.6c03425](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsapm.6c03425)
**Relevance Score**: 2.59/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The study addresses the inherent limitations of solid polymer electrolytes (SPEs) in lithium metal batteries, specifically their low lithium-ion transference numbers, poor interfacial stability with high-voltage cathodes, and susceptibility to lithium dendrite growth.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim the development of a composite electrolyte (FALET) consisting of a polymer, an ionic liquid (EMIMTFSI), and TiO2 nanofibers. They propose that this combination synergistically regulates ion transport and interfacial chemistry, specifically through Lewis acid-base interactions between TiO2 and TFSI- anions to promote salt dissociation and homogenize ion flux.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that the FALET electrolyte achieves an ionic conductivity of 6.52 × 10–4 S cm–1, a transference number of 0.31, and an electrochemical stability window of 5.01 V. They report that these properties facilitate stable lithium plating/stripping for 2700 hours and improved performance in full-cell configurations, suggesting the composite effectively suppresses dendrite formation and enhances thermal safety.

**Relevance Score**: 2.38/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The reliance of conventional all-solid-state lithium battery cathodes on excessive inactive solid electrolyte fillers (20-40%), which limits active material density, creates tortuous ion transport paths, and promotes unstable heterogeneous interfaces.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

A systematic review of catholyte-free cathode architectures across oxide, sulfide, and halide systems, including a categorization of material optimization strategies such as defect modulation, amorphization, and composite structural design.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that catholyte-free designs offer superior energy density and cycling stability by replacing heterogeneous CAM/SE interfaces with homogeneous CAM/CAM interfaces, and they provide a framework for future optimization of these architectures.
