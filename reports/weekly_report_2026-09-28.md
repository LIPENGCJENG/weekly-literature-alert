## 1. Synergistic Spatiotemporal Regulation of Li + ‐Flux During Formation Enables Stable Solid‐Electrolyte Interphase for Ah‐Level Si/C Anode‐Based Quasi‐Solid‐State Lithium Batteries

**Authors**: Qianqian Guo, Xianlei Shen, Yunyun Zhai, Yanyan Ma, Qian Liu, Rui Luo et al.
**Journal/Platform**: Small
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 12.1 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-09-27
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1002/smll.76038](https://doi.org/10.1002/smll.76038)
**Relevance Score**: 2.94/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The instability of the solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) in Si/C anode-based quasi-solid-state batteries, caused by spatial heterogeneity and temporal Li+-flux accumulation during standard constant-current formation processes.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors propose a dual-strategy approach: spatially regulating Li+-flux via a piezoelectric BaTiO3/PVDF-b-PTFE gel polymer electrolyte to create a uniform local polarization field, and temporally regulating Li+-flux via a bipolar pulse formation protocol to allow for ion relaxation.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that the synergistic spatiotemporal regulation produces a denser, more uniform, and higher-modulus SEI, which they claim leads to improved interfacial stability and extended cycle life in Ah-level NCM811||Si/C full cells.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The sluggish lithium-ion mobility in poly(ethylene oxide) (PEO) electrolytes caused by strong chelation between Li+ and ether oxygens, combined with insufficient lithium salt dissociation.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors introduce phosphonate-functionalized polycarboxylate ether (P-PCE) as a functional additive to PEO, claiming it facilitates a multi-Li+ coordination mechanism that destabilizes Li+ solvation, promotes salt dissociation, and suppresses PEO crystallization.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The incorporation of P-PCE enables an ionic conductivity of 1.7 × 10−4 S cm−1 at 30°C and supports stable cycling in Li||LiFePO4 cells at room temperature by simultaneously increasing Li+ concentration and mobility through transient, high-entropy coordination environments.

## 5. “Lithium‐Free” Anode Solid‐State Batteries Enabled by 3D Structured Miec Garnet

**Authors**: G. V. Alexander, Aoife M. Zuercher, Jay Yvonne Easter, Christopher Tang, William Schubert, J. Evans Gritton et al.
**Journal/Platform**: Advanced Energy Materials
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 26 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-09-27
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.71648](https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.71648)
**Relevance Score**: 1.3/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The authors address the challenge of inhomogeneous lithium nucleation and unstable growth at the current collector-solid electrolyte interface in 'lithium-free' solid-state batteries, which typically results in poor cycling reversibility and high anode impedance.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to enable uniform lithium nucleation by utilizing a 3D porous mixed ionic and electronic conducting (MIEC) garnet scaffold, which facilitates electronic connectivity at low lithium volume fractions and reduces anode impedance.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The study concludes that the MIEC scaffold transforms lithium nucleation into a percolation-governed transport process, achieving a reported Coulombic efficiency of 99.95% and enabling stable, pressure-free cycling of full cells at room temperature when paired with NMC811 cathodes.
