## 1. Cationic chain-anchored monolayer enables wide-temperature composite polymer electrolytes for lithium-metal batteries

**Authors**: Qimin Peng, Yong Chen, Jiajun Gong, Haojie Xu, Taolue Wen, Mengyu Liu et al.
**Journal/Platform**: Nature Communications
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 18.9 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-10-02
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78048-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78048-0)
**Relevance Score**: 5.61/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The inability of conventional composite polymer electrolytes (specifically PVDF-HFP/garnet systems) to simultaneously provide fast lithium-ion transport kinetics and maintain stable electrolyte-electrode interfaces across extreme wide-temperature ranges (-25 °C to 120 °C).

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim an interfacial engineering strategy using an in situ-formed cationic chain-anchored monolayer featuring solvent-phobic, chain-tethered cationic channels that establish single-ion-like conducting pathways, characterized via synchrotron infrared spectroscopy, 2D solid-state NMR, and operando diagnostics.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that structuring the composite interface with this cationic monolayer lowers the activation energy to 0.092 eV and increases the Li+ transference number to 0.78, which reportedly enables stable cycling under both high-temperature (120 °C, >99% retention over 400 cycles in Li||LiFePO4) and low-temperature/high-voltage (-25 °C, 82.5% retention over 450 cycles in 4.5 V Li||NCM811) conditions.

## 2. Fire‐Safe Hierarchical Solid Polymer Electrolytes Enabled by Dual‐Functional Microcapsules for Lithium Metal Batteries

**Authors**: Ziqi Huang, Jiuqing Gui, Qiaoying Cao, Yong Xiao, Hang Hu, Yeru Liang
**Journal/Platform**: Advanced Science
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 15.6 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-10-04
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.78034](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.78034)
**Relevance Score**: 5.45/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The study targets the limitation of conventional solid-state electrolytes that rely merely on passive, intrinsic nonflammability, rendering them ineffective at actively extinguishing or suppressing combustion once thermal runaway is initiated in lithium metal batteries.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim a hierarchical solid polymer electrolyte (HSPE) incorporating dual-functional microcapsules—composed of a perfluoro(2-methyl-3-pentanone) fire-extinguishing core inside an ammonium hydrogen phytate-modified urea-formaldehyde shell, embedded in an ethylene-vinyl acetate matrix—which provides active agent release and protective char formation upon thermal rupture while reportedly preserving an ionic conductivity of 0.64 mS cm⁻¹ and an electrochemical stability window up to 4.6 V.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that incorporating dual-functional, thermally responsive microcapsules into solid polymer electrolytes successfully suppresses combustion and prevents ignition in pouch cells subjected to thermal abuse, demonstrating that active fire-extinguishing capabilities can be integrated into solid-state battery electrolytes without sacrificing ambient electrochemical performance.

## 4. High-Conductivity and Moisture-Tolerant Sulfide Electrolyte Enabled by Bifunctional Coating for Practical All-Solid-State Lithium Batteries

**Authors**: Dabing Li, Hongjie Xu, Guoqin Cao, Fanfan Liu, Jinjin Ban, Shilin Zhang et al.
**Journal/Platform**: Nano-Micro Letters
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 38.5 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-10-05
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1007/s40820-026-02353-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40820-026-02353-y)
**Relevance Score**: 4.26/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

Sulfide solid electrolytes, specifically Li5.5PS4.5Cl0.8Br0.7, suffer from acute moisture sensitivity that triggers rapid structural degradation and toxic H2S gas release, presenting a major barrier to processing and manufacturing all-solid-state batteries in ambient or dry-room conditions without compromising ionic transport.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim a surface-passivation strategy using a ~10 nm bifunctional (3-mercaptopropyl)trimethoxysilane (MPTMS) coating on Li5.5PS4.5Cl0.8Br0.7 to provide a hydrophobic shield against moisture ingress while maintaining high bulk-like lithium-ion conductivity.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The evidence presented indicates that the MPTMS layer effectively mitigates degradation under controlled exposure, reducing H2S release by 93.0% after 35 minutes at 30% relative humidity, preserving ionic conductivity above 5.7 mS cm⁻¹ after 10 hours in air, and enabling a full cell with a LiNi0.83Co0.11Mn0.06O2 cathode to maintain 84.6% capacity after 1000 cycles; however, claims regarding full ambient air compatibility remain bounded by the specific moderate humidity and exposure durations evaluated.

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

Elucidating the molecular-level origins of the differing transport kinetics between Li+ and Na+ in polymer electrolytes, specifically addressing why macroscopic cation mobility is poorly captured by static, spatially averaged coordination numbers.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

A hybrid computational framework combining atomistic molecular dynamics and quantum mechanical calculations to formulate a population-weighted interaction energy descriptor (⟨Eint⟩), which combines Boltzmann-averaged coordination motif energies with MD-derived state populations across 14 PEO and 4 PEC formulations.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

Simulations demonstrate that higher Na+ mobility relative to Li+ in PEO stems from a more diffuse and dynamic coordination shell compared to the compact, persistent cages binding Li+; while the population-weighted energetic descriptor correlates with the cation contribution to conductivity within PEO, it fails to transfer directly to PEC where matrix rigidity and polymer segmental dynamics significantly confound local coordination energetics.
