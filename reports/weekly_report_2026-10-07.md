## 1. Poly(propylene carbonate- co -thioether- co -ether) Terpolymer and Its Application as a Solid-State Electrolyte

**Authors**: Shun Ai, Xiaofeng Hao, Xianli Wu, Jintao Wang, Xu Li, Yuezhong Meng
**Journal/Platform**: ACS Applied Polymer Materials
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 5.2 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-10-06
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1021/acsapm.6c03271](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsapm.6c03271)
**Relevance Score**: 4.93/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The controllable, metal-free catalytic synthesis of main-chain sulfur-containing polymers, which are structurally desirable for solid-state electrolyte applications but traditionally difficult to synthesize without metal catalysts.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim a one-step, metal-free catalytic copolymerization strategy combining carbon dioxide, epoxides, and ethylene sulfide to prepare poly(propylene carbonate-co-thioether-co-ether) (PPCTE). They demonstrate tuning of monomer ratios and catalyst selection to isolate a 27 kg mol–1 terpolymer containing 5% sulfur segments in the main chain (PPCTE0.05) and evaluate its properties as a solid-state polymer electrolyte.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that introducing sulfur segments via this metal-free route yields a mechanically flexible (647% elongation at break), low-Tg (5.8 °C) polymer electrolyte with acceptable room-temperature ionic conductivity (5.36 × 10–5 S cm–1), low activation energy (0.29 eV), and a wide electrochemical stability window (5.21 V). However, broader claims regarding the method's economic and eco-friendly viability, as well as its general utility in battery cells, remain preliminary author assertions supported only by benchtop synthesis parameters and basic electrochemical characterizations provided in the abstract.

## 3. Indispensable Interphase Passivation in Solvation‐Regulated Propylene Carbonate Electrolytes for Ah‐Level Lithium‐Ion Batteries at −50–120°C

**Authors**: Haijin Ji, Yaqi Liao, Zezhuo Li, Zhangyating Xie, Yanda Li, Lin Du et al.
**Journal/Platform**: Advanced Functional Materials
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 19.9 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-10-07
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1002/adfm.78833](https://doi.org/10.1002/adfm.78833)
**Relevance Score**: 0.99/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The paper addresses whether an engineered solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) is fundamentally indispensable alongside solvation-structure tuning to prevent catastrophic Li⁺–propylene carbonate (PC) co-intercalation in graphite anodes, particularly under demanding high-current regimes and wide operational temperature ranges.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim to decouple the mechanistic roles of bulk solvation regulation and interphase passivation by employing PFPN to weaken Li⁺–PC coordination and FEC to generate a protective SEI. They demonstrate this dual strategy in 1.0 Ah Gr||NCM83 pouch cells, claiming an initial Coulombic efficiency of 93.5%, stable cycling beyond 2,000 cycles, and functionality across an extreme temperature range of −50 °C to 120 °C.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

Solvation regulation alone fails at higher charge/discharge rates because elevated cathodic overpotentials drive Li⁺–PC co-intercalation at exposed graphite facets. Consequently, a continuous, passivating SEI acts as an indispensable transport barrier to exclude Li⁺–PC complexes, proving that both interphase passivation and solvation tuning are necessary to achieve stable, high-rate, wide-temperature graphite-based lithium-ion battery cycling.

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

Current modeling approaches for polymer-based battery electrolytes are largely fragmented into isolated models, failing to capture how formulation and processing history dictate multi-scale structure, interfacial behavior, and performance due to persistent gaps in material-state definition, process coupling, and experiment-model information exchange.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

### 3. What are its main conclusions?
