## 1. In Situ Cross-Linked Solid Polymer Electrolytes with Synergistic Dissociation–Coordination Modulation for Stable Solid-State Lithium Batteries

**Authors**: Yi Zhang, Caiqian Liang, Yu Cheng, Yu Dou, Jiandong Gong, Yalong Jiang et al.
**Journal/Platform**: ACS Sustainable Chemistry & Engineering
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 7.8 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-10-17
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.6c08147](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.6c08147)
**Relevance Score**: 7.39/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The paper addresses two interconnected trade-offs in solid polymer electrolytes for lithium batteries: the conflict between promoting lithium salt dissociation and minimizing Li+ coordination barriers to facilitate transport, coupled with the classic compromise between achieving sufficient mechanical durability and maintaining fast Li+ transport kinetics.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim an in situ cross-linked copolymer electrolyte (designated TGDODT) that integrates a crosslinking center to enhance salt dissociation with thioether units to weaken Li+ coordination. They claim this architecture concurrently provides flame retardancy, thermal and mechanical integrity, a three-dimensional continuous Li+ pathway, and the ability to induce an inorganic solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) to suppress lithium dendrites.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that the molecular design resolves transport and stability bottlenecks, reporting an ionic conductivity of 1.31 × 10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹, a high Li+ transference number of 0.75, and stable Li||TGDODT||LFP cycling (88.2% capacity retention after 660 cycles at 1 C). While the electrochemical transport and cycling metrics are quantitatively reported, claims regarding flame retardancy, specific inorganic SEI formation, and suppression of dendrites are asserted as design benefits without detailed quantitative evidence provided in the available text.

## 3. Electrochemical and Mechanical Understanding of Extruded and Solvent‐Cast Solid Polymer Electrolytes

**Authors**: Elene Sasieta-Barrutia, June Blanco, Laida Otaegui, Aizeti Burgoa, Mikel Isasi, Michel B. Armand et al.
**Journal/Platform**: Advanced Energy Materials
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 26 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-10-16
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.71697](https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.71697)
**Relevance Score**: 5.97/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

Resolving the coupled electrochemical and mechanical trade-offs in PEO–LiTFSI solid polymer electrolytes, specifically identifying how distinct manufacturing routes (extrusion vs. solvent casting), inert filler addition (Al2O3), and membrane thickness govern ion transport kinetics, polarization gradients, and mechanical vulnerability to dendrite penetration.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

A systematic comparative evaluation of PEO–LiTFSI membranes fabricated via extrusion and solvent casting, detailing how Al2O3 filler loading and membrane thickness directly alter mechanical parameters (storage modulus, yield stress, fracture toughness) alongside electrochemical properties (diffusion rates, Sand times, polarization), validated through multilayer cell assembly.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that extruded membranes deliver superior stiffness and dendrite resistance compared to solvent-cast films (attributed by the authors to processing-induced polymer chain shortening), Al2O3 incorporation enhances fracture toughness and interface stability, and minimizing membrane thickness mitigates polarization and diffusion bottlenecks; consequently, thin, Al2O3-filled extruded membranes are asserted to offer the optimal trade-off for solid-state battery integration.

## 4. Competitive Coordination to Ether Oxygens Enables High‐Ionic‐Conductivity Solid Polymer Electrolytes at Room Temperature

**Authors**: Chengru Chen, Hanpei Liu, Zixuan Tang, Tao Sun, Qiahan Liu, Zihao Xue et al.
**Journal/Platform**: Small Methods
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 9.5 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-10-13
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1002/smtd.71118](https://doi.org/10.1002/smtd.71118)
**Relevance Score**: 5.14/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The practical deployment of poly(ethylene oxide) (PEO)-based electrolytes in solid-state lithium metal batteries is fundamentally bottlenecked by poor ionic conductivity at ambient temperature (25 °C), driven by strong Li⁺–ether oxygen coordination and restricted lithium-salt dissociation.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim a coordination-tuning approach via introducing FeF3 as a multifunctional additive into a PEO/PVDF-HFP blend. Mechanistically, they claim that Lewis-acidic Fe³⁺ competitively coordinates with ether oxygens to weaken Li⁺–O–C binding (reducing the Li⁺ hopping barrier) while concurrently destabilizing the Li–N interaction in LiTFSI to enhance salt dissociation and free Li⁺ carrier concentration.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that competitive Lewis-acid coordination successfully circumvents PEO's room-temperature transport bottleneck. Based on reported metrics, this strategy yields an ambient ionic conductivity of 0.13 mS cm⁻¹ at 25 °C and enables solid-state Li||LiFePO4 cells to operate at 25 °C (0.5 C) with an initial discharge capacity of 120.6 mAh g⁻¹ and 88.4% capacity retention across 1000 cycles.

## 5. Bi/K/La co-substituted BaTiO3 ceramic fillers for enhanced PDMS-based triboelectric nanogenerators

**Authors**: Kuilin Chen, Min Zhang, Wei Wen
**Journal/Platform**: Materials Science and Engineering B
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 44.7 / JCR not available(配置表)
**Publication Date**: 2026-10-17
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mseb.2026.119931](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mseb.2026.119931)
**Relevance Score**: 4.57/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

The limited dielectric response of polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) triboelectric layers, which restricts the electrical output and temperature stability of PDMS-based triboelectric nanogenerators (TENGs), and the inadequacy of conventional approaches that rely solely on adjusting high-permittivity filler loading rather than tailoring the intrinsic structural and dielectric properties of the ceramic fillers.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim a compositional design strategy involving Bi/K/La co-substitution into BaTiO3 (yielding Ba1-xBi0.5x-0.02K0.5xLa0.02TiO3) to intrinsically regulate perovskite crystal structure, phase stability, and relaxor dielectric characteristics, thereby shifting filler optimization from simple loading variation to intrinsic lattice- and property-level design for PDMS-based contact-separation TENGs.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The evidence indicates that TENG output does not correlate monotonically with permittivity magnitude or relaxor degree; rather, an intermediate composition (BBKLT-0.4 at 10 wt% loading) achieves an empirical balance of perovskite phase stability, low dielectric loss, and pronounced relaxor behavior. Under tested contact-separation conditions, this yields an open-circuit voltage of 156.14 V, short-circuit current of 7.07 µA, and power density of ~476.2 mW/m2 with demonstrated stability from -10 to 60 °C; however, because only three discrete stoichiometry values (x = 0.2, 0.4, 0.6) were evaluated, the claim represents an empirical composition optimum rather than a thoroughly mapped continuous design framework.

## 6. Zr-based oxyhalide superionic electrolyte with self-passivating interphase stabilisation for high-voltage and fast-charging all-solid-state lithium batteries

**Journal/Platform**: Nature Communications
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 18.9 / Q1(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-10-17
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78813-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78813-1)
**Relevance Score**: 3.28/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

Solid electrolytes with high ionic conductivity are typically intrinsically unstable at high potentials (decomposing above 4.30 V vs Li+/Li), causing severe interfacial degradation, impedance growth, and poor cyclability when integrated with high-voltage cathodes in fast-charging all-solid-state lithium batteries.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim a 'non-equimolar cation-anion amorphous engineering' design on Li2ZrCl6 to produce an amorphous oxyhalide electrolyte (Li2Zr0.625Ta0.3Cl4.2O0.9). They claim this material reconciles superionic conductivity (4.11 mS cm−1 at 25 °C) with high-voltage operational tolerance mediated by a self-passivating positive electrode–electrolyte interphase.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

The authors conclude that amorphous oxyhalide engineering successfully mitigates cathode-electrolyte interfacial breakdown under high-voltage and fast-rate conditions. This conclusion is supported by reported test data showing a LiNi0.88Mn0.06Co0.06O2 || Li-In cell retaining 81.2% capacity after 2,000 cycles at 10 C up to a 4.18 V cell cutoff (equivalent to 4.80 V vs Li+/Li); however, claims regarding the mechanistic nature of the self-passivating interphase are inferred primarily from electrochemical stability metrics.

## 7. Crosslinker‐Free Stabilization of Poly(vinyl Alcohol)‐Poly(acrylic Acid) Gel Polymer Electrolytes Using Multifunctional Additives for Rechargeable Zinc–Air Batteries

**Authors**: Alejandro Arredondo‐Espínola, Carlos Manuel Ramos-Castillo, Beatriz Liliana España‐Sánchez, Lorena Álvarez‐Contreras, Noé Arjona
**Journal/Platform**: Batteries & Supercaps
**SCI Impact Factor / JCR Quartile**: 4.9 / Q2(EasyScholar)
**Publication Date**: 2026-10-18
**DOI**: [https://doi.org/10.1002/batt.70513](https://doi.org/10.1002/batt.70513)
**Relevance Score**: 2.58/10

### 1. What problem is it really trying to solve?

Conventional stabilization of gel polymer electrolytes (GPEs) for zinc–air batteries typically relies on chemical crosslinkers or thermal curing, which increases processing complexity and can impede ionic transport.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim a crosslinker-free, room-temperature stabilization approach for PVA–PAA hydrogel electrolytes using low-molecular-weight polyols (glycerol and ethylene glycol) via hydrogen-bond engineering. They claim this eliminates the need for thermal curing while outperforming thermally treated baselines in ionic conductivity, alkaline electrolyte uptake, and high-temperature tolerance.

### 3. What are its main conclusions?

Incorporating glycerol yields a polymer network with high KOH retention (1080%) and high ionic conductivity (208 mS cm⁻¹), enabling stable zinc–air battery cycling for 500 cycles (80 h) and operation up to 85 °C. However, the evidence also reflects an inherent mechanical trade-off, as elongation at break decreases from 121% to 82% compared to the thermally cured benchmark.

Developing poly(ethylene oxide)-based gel polymer electrolytes that possess suitable rheological properties for ambient-air screen-printing while simultaneously maintaining adequate room-temperature ionic transport and interfacial electrochemical stability against lithium-metal anodes and high-voltage cathodes.

### 2. What contribution does it claim?

The authors claim the formulation of an ambient-air screen-printable gel polymer electrolyte (PEO-LiTFSI-TEGDME with DIOX or ACN processing solvents and optional succinonitrile additive) that enables ambient-air deposition and drying, achieves room-temperature ionic conductivity of 1.34 × 10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹ with a Li⁺ transference number up to 0.65, and exhibits an apparent two-electrode stability limit of 4.57 V.
